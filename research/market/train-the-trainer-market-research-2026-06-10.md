# Train-the-Trainer Market Research - 2026-06-10

## Scope

Research question: should CyberSkills Academy validate a parallel B2B/B2B2C product that licenses certification-prep courseware, labs, mock exams, and trainer-prep assets to instructors, training centers, universities, bootcamps, and consultants?

This track is additive. It does not replace the learner-facing Academy.

## Executive verdict

The idea is worth validating because it changes the buyer economics. A direct learner buys a course to improve themselves; an instructor or training center buys a kit to generate revenue. That makes willingness-to-pay potentially higher and gives Academy distribution leverage: one instructor can bring many learners.

The strongest position is not "another certification course" and not "unofficial copy of official courseware." The strongest position is:

> Commercially licensed, teaching-ready cybersecurity courseware for local instructors and small training providers: updated content, labs, mock exams with explanations, trainer-prep videos, launch assets, and clear legal usage rights.

The first validation should be a paid pilot or deposit for one course/module, not a platform build.

## Market signal

### Demand for cyber training remains structurally strong

- Fortinet's 2025 skills gap report says 89% of surveyed decision makers prefer to hire candidates with certifications, and 73% would pay for an employee to obtain a cybersecurity certification.
- ISC2 partnered with Thailand's NCSA in 2024 to offer 10,000 training and certification opportunities over two years for the ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity credential.
- Public market estimates show Thailand IT training as a large existing category, not a greenfield bet. IMARC estimates Thailand's IT training market at USD 517.1M in 2025 and USD 668.3M by 2034.
- Global cyber training/certification markets are still projected to grow, but the useful signal for Academy is narrower: buyers already budget for certification training, and local instructors/training centers need differentiated teaching assets.

### The direct-to-learner market is crowded

The B2C side has official materials, books, low-cost videos, Udemy-style courses, YouTube creators, practice exams, and bootcamps. That does not kill Academy, but it makes distribution and trust the binding constraint.

The train-the-trainer wedge avoids competing only for each individual learner. It sells production capacity to people who already own or can create learner demand.

## Buyer segments

| Segment | Job to be done | WTP signal | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Independent instructors / consultants | Launch a paid cert-prep class quickly | Medium | Most price-sensitive, but fastest to validate by paid pilot. |
| Small training centers | Add a new cybersecurity course to catalog | High | Strongest fit if they already sell corporate/group training. |
| Universities / vocational programs | Add current curriculum without building from zero | Medium-high | Procurement slower, but recurring adoption possible. |
| Corporate L&D / security teams | Run internal upskilling with instructor facilitation | High | May not need commercial resale rights; needs reporting and labs. |
| Government / workforce programs | Scale cyber workforce training | High but slow | Requires compliance, references, formal partner posture. |

## Customer pain points

1. Content authoring from zero is slow and expensive.
2. Certification versions change; instructors need transition guides and updated content.
3. Labs are difficult to provision, support, and keep reproducible.
4. New instructors need confidence: scripts, timing plans, common questions, facilitation guidance.
5. Training centers need sales assets, course outlines, and commercial usage rights.
6. Credibility is fragile: wrong answer keys, outdated objectives, or trademark misuse can damage both instructor and provider.

## Competitor landscape

### 1. Official certification ecosystems

| Competitor | What they provide | Strength | Weakness / opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| CompTIA | Official courseware, instructor resources, CertMaster products, CIN community | Official status, exam alignment, instructor resources such as slides, planners, transition guides | Access depends on roles/contracts/partner path. Less localized, less "launch your teaching business" oriented. Trademark rules constrain positioning. |
| ISC2 | Official training partners, authorized instructors, official trainer materials and lesson plans | Strong authority for CISSP/CCSP/CC/SSCP | Official partner path is controlled. If CYBERSKILLS is not an OTP, cannot imply official status. |
| EC-Council | CEI instructor portal, slides, labs, setup guides, videos, Academia partner resources | Very close to the proposed model | Authorized ecosystem and brand control. Also expensive and sometimes reputationally polarizing in security communities. |
| Cisco Networking Academy | Educator community, curriculum, labs, instructor training | Huge educator network and strong entry-level funnel | Institution-oriented; not a flexible commercial kit for independent non-Cisco cert courses. |

Takeaway: the category exists. Official players validate the bundle shape: slides, instructor guide, labs, trainer prep, transition/update resources. CYBERSKILLS must avoid pretending to be official unless formally authorized.

### 2. Courseware and curriculum providers

| Competitor | What they provide | Strength | Weakness / opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logical Operations | IT courseware, CHOICE platform, lab integrations, training partner support | Mature courseware provider, broad catalog, many delivery partners | Broad/generic. Pricing often sales-led. Local security depth and Thai/ASEAN context are openings. |
| Pearson IT Cybersecurity Curriculum | Turnkey cybersecurity curriculum with instructor supplements and uCertify lab/course tie-ins | Academic fit, recognized publisher | More degree/curriculum oriented than "new instructor revenue kit." |
| uCertify | 1000+ courses, labs, test prep, LMS integration, authoring/proctoring tools | Platform breadth and academic partnerships | Can feel generic/long-form; less local commercial launch support. |

Takeaway: these vendors compete if a school/training center wants generic digital courseware. CYBERSKILLS should differentiate on instructor monetization, local market adaptation, up-to-date security expertise, better facilitation assets, and high-quality explained answers.

### 3. Lab and cyber range providers

| Competitor | What they provide | Strength | Weakness / opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skillable | White-label virtual labs, partner enablement, hands-on labs for ILT/VILT/self-paced | Strong infrastructure for scalable labs | Not full cert-prep courseware or commercial teaching kit by itself. |
| ACI Learning / Practice Labs | Academic and workforce lab sets | Ready-made lab layer | Lab-centric; not full local instructor business system. |
| RangeForce / Immersive Labs / Hack The Box / TryHackMe | Skills labs, ranges, role-based training | Strong hands-on practice and brand awareness | Usually sold as learner/team platform, not instructor commercial courseware license. |

Takeaway: partner or integrate where possible. Building lab infrastructure too early would violate validate-before-invest.

### 4. Training providers and bootcamps

Local and global providers already sell instructor-led certification prep. Thai public examples show wide price dispersion:

- Network Training Center lists CompTIA Security+ at THB 47,000 plus VAT for 5 days.
- SterlingNext Bangkok lists CompTIA Security+ live online around THB 54,840 and classroom around THB 130,895.
- Sprintzeal Bangkok lists Security+ classroom pricing above THB 120,000.
- Certstaff lists public instructor-led Security+ from USD 2,330 per student.
- The Knowledge Academy lists CompTIA training fees in Thailand from USD 4,195.

Takeaway: training providers are both competitors and potential buyers. If a center can sell a 10-person cohort, a high-quality content kit can pay back in one cohort.

## Pricing anchors

| Anchor | Observed market range | Implication |
|---|---:|---|
| Self-study cert prep | Often tens to low hundreds USD; very price competitive | Do not price the trainer kit like B2C content. |
| Individual courseware/labs | Around USD 100-700+ depending bundle/vendor/cert | Student-seat add-ons must be affordable or bundled. |
| Instructor-led Security+ training | THB 47,000 to 130,000+ locally; USD 2,330+ in US examples | Training centers can absorb licensing if it helps sell cohorts. |
| Official/authorized advanced training | Can be several thousand USD per learner | Official trust is expensive; CYBERSKILLS must compete with quality, locality, and speed, not official claims. |
| Lab/range platforms | Often quote-based or high annual per-user/team pricing | Labs should be optional variable pricing during validation. |

## Recommended pricing model

Use a hybrid license + subscription + lab-seat model.

### Pilot pricing

Goal: validate money commitment, not maximize revenue.

| Offer | Price | Includes | Gate |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Paid discovery pilot | THB 15,000-25,000 | 1 module sample, instructor guide, slide excerpt, mock sample, feedback call | Good if 3+ buyers pay quickly. |
| First cohort license | THB 35,000-60,000 | One course/module kit for one instructor and one cohort, no public redistribution | Best validation of commercial use. |
| Design partner annual | THB 75,000-120,000 | One course, updates for 12 months, 2-3 instructor seats, limited student material rights | Use only after pilot demand. |

### Public packaging after validation

| Tier | Annual price target | Fit | Rights |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Solo Instructor | THB 49,000-89,000 per course/year | Independent trainer | 1 instructor, limited cohort count or student-seat cap, no resale. |
| Training Center | THB 150,000-300,000 per course/year | Small provider | 3 instructors, larger student-seat cap, branded delivery under license, quarterly updates. |
| Institution | THB 300,000-750,000 per course/year | University/corporate/government | Multi-instructor, reporting, procurement support, optional custom mapping. |
| Catalog Partner | Quote-based | Multi-course partner | Revenue share or annual minimum guarantee. |

### Lab seat pricing

Keep labs separate so content licensing does not carry compute risk.

| Lab type | Suggested charge | Use case |
|---|---:|---|
| Lightweight guided labs | THB 800-1,500 per learner/cohort | Browser/simple VM/sandbox tasks. |
| Standard cert-prep lab pack | THB 1,500-3,500 per learner/cohort | Security+ / fundamentals style labs. |
| Advanced cyber range pack | THB 3,500-8,000+ per learner/cohort | SecurityX, pentest, DFIR, cloud security. |

If using a third-party lab platform, price as pass-through plus margin. If building internal labs later, set a gross margin target after measuring actual compute/support cost.

## Business model

### Revenue streams

1. Annual commercial content license.
2. Per-student or per-cohort lab seats.
3. Instructor onboarding / certification workshop.
4. Content update subscription.
5. Custom localization or corporate adaptation.
6. Optional QA/approved-instructor tier.
7. Enterprise/institution site license.

### License boundaries

The commercial license should explicitly define:

- Authorized instructor count.
- Authorized student-seat count or cohort count.
- Whether students can receive PDF/print/digital copies.
- No resale or redistribution of source files.
- No public upload to open LMS, file shares, or marketplaces.
- No use of certification-provider logos unless independently authorized.
- No claim that CYBERSKILLS content is official unless formally authorized.
- No use of actual exam questions, leaked questions, or braindumps.
- Audit/right-to-terminate clause for misuse.
- Update entitlement and version cutoff.

### Unit economics

Content subscription can have high gross margin after authoring, but only if support is controlled. Labs and QA can consume margin quickly.

Target model:

- Content/update subscription gross margin: 75-90% after first version.
- Lab seats: price only after measuring per-seat infrastructure and support.
- Instructor support: cap included support hours by tier; charge for extra onboarding/customization.
- Maintenance budget: assume every cert course needs planned refresh cycles, not ad hoc emergency updates.

## Product strategy

### Best first wedge

Do not start with a full catalog. Start with one course/module where CYBERSKILLS has credibility and can produce a high-quality kit quickly.

Recommended candidates:

1. Security fundamentals / Security+ aligned teaching kit - largest trainer demand, but trademark/legal wording must be clean.
2. Cybersecurity for AI / Agentic AI security - less official-cert dependency, stronger thought-leadership angle, faster differentiation.
3. SecurityX/CAS-005 - useful existing asset, but not first until answer-key disputes are resolved and advanced-market demand is proven.
4. ISO/risk/GRC short course - easier commercial training market, lower lab complexity, corporate-friendly.

Preferred first validation offer:

> "Security+ aligned instructor starter kit for Thai/ASEAN trainers" or "AI Security instructor starter kit," depending on which channel has reachable prospects first.

The choice should be driven by 10-20 real prospect conversations, not by content preference.

### MVP deliverable

One module sample is enough:

- 20-30 slide deck.
- Instructor notes/script.
- 90-120 minute lesson plan.
- 1 hands-on lab or tabletop exercise.
- 20-question mock quiz with explained answers.
- Student handout.
- Version/update note.
- One-page commercial license summary.
- Sales one-pager for the instructor/training center.
- 15-30 minute trainer-prep video.

## Go-to-market

### Prospect list

Start with reachable buyers:

1. Founder academic network.
2. Existing CYBERSKILLS corporate contacts.
3. Thai training centers already selling CompTIA/Cisco/EC-Council/ISC2 courses.
4. University/vocational cyber program instructors.
5. Independent instructors active on LinkedIn/Facebook communities.
6. Security consultants who already do workshops.

### Validation sequence

1. Interview 10-20 prospects.
2. Ask what course they want to launch in the next 90 days.
3. Show the one-module sample and license summary.
4. Test price points: THB 25k pilot, THB 59k first cohort, THB 99k annual design partner.
5. Require money, LOI, or scheduled paid pilot.
6. Record objections: legal trust, student demand, price, lab availability, update promise, language.

### Go / no-go thresholds

Proceed to a larger kit only if at least one of these happens within 30-45 days:

- 3 paid pilots, or
- 2 training centers sign LOIs with named cohort dates, or
- 1 institution/corporate buyer pays for custom pilot, or
- 5+ credible instructors request the same course and at least 2 accept a paid deposit.

Stop or reframe if:

- Prospects like the idea but refuse paid pilot/deposit.
- Legal constraints prevent clear commercial usage.
- Lab cost/support makes gross margin unattractive.
- Buyers only want official courseware and will not accept "aligned but independent."

## Competitive positioning

### Positioning statement

CyberSkills Academy helps cybersecurity instructors and training providers launch high-quality, commercially licensed training faster by supplying updated courseware, labs, explained mocks, trainer-prep videos, and local delivery assets.

### Differentiators

- Thai/ASEAN localization and commercial teaching context.
- Instructor-first design: scripts, timing, common questions, facilitation notes.
- Explained answers as a quality marker.
- Integrated labs without forcing a full LMS platform decision.
- Clear commercial license, not ambiguous "download and hope."
- CYBERSKILLS credibility across security services, labs, and corporate training.

### What not to claim

- Do not claim official authorization unless formally authorized.
- Do not imply pass guarantee.
- Do not use certification-provider marks as product names.
- Do not market with "real exam questions," "actual questions," or similar language.
- Do not make public distribution until answer-key disputes are resolved for any reused bank.

## Impact assessment

| Dimension | Upside | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| QoS | Higher quality bar because instructors reuse content across cohorts | Bad instructors can damage CYBERSKILLS brand | License terms, approved-instructor tier, feedback loop. |
| Performance / cost | Higher revenue per buyer than B2C | Support and lab operations can become heavy | Start with sample kit and optional labs; cap support. |
| Security / legal | Clear license can reduce misuse | Trademark, official-status, braindump, redistribution risk | Legal review, no official claims, no leaked exam content, audit terms. |
| UX | Faster time-to-teach for buyers | Too many assets can overwhelm instructors | Package by delivery flow: before class, during class, after class. |

Tradeoff: the track adds legal and QA burden, but the distribution leverage and higher WTP justify a cheap validation cycle.

## Known unknowns

- Actual Thai/ASEAN willingness-to-pay for independent instructor licensing.
- Whether target buyers prefer English, Thai, or bilingual materials.
- Which first course has the strongest channel pull.
- Third-party lab provider cost and resale terms.
- Whether CYBERSKILLS should pursue official partner status for selected certifications later.
- Maintenance hours per course version update.

## Recommended next actions

1. Keep the learner-facing Academy plan unchanged.
2. Add a Train-the-Trainer validation card for one-module sample + prospect interviews.
3. Pick 10-20 prospects before choosing the first course.
4. Draft a plain-English commercial license summary before any sample is sent out.
5. Do not build a platform or subscribe to a paid LMS/lab platform until a paid pilot exists.
